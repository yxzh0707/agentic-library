## Config delta vs baseline-0

```
+ --split_mode timestamp
```

Plus `dataset.py` and `train.py` gain three split-mode branches
(`rowgroup` / `timestamp` / `random_seed`) and the `PCVRParquetDataset`
constructor accepts new `timestamp_min` / `timestamp_max` /
`row_group_indices` kwargs. Default `split_mode='rowgroup'` keeps the
upstream baseline behavior byte-identical.

## Mechanism

`get_pcvr_data` in timestamp mode performs a one-pass scan of every
parquet's `timestamp` column, computes the `(1 - valid_ratio)` quantile
as a threshold, then constructs:

- `train_dataset` with `timestamp_max=threshold` (rows < threshold)
- `valid_dataset` with `timestamp_min=threshold` (rows ≥ threshold)

Per-batch row filtering happens at the start of `_convert_batch`; rejected batches return `None` and `__iter__` skips them. No parquet rewrite, no `USER_CACHE_PATH` use (which is 20 GiB cap < 41 GiB data).

## Why this experiment

`pcvr_diag_data_split` (2026-04-30) revealed TencentGR's 1000 parquet shards each span the same ~4.6-day window — they are sample-shuffled, not time-partitioned. The upstream `get_pcvr_data` "last 10% of files alphabetically" therefore yields a uniformly-random in-distribution validation set, not a temporal hold-out. Baseline-0's val_auc 0.86 vs leaderboard 0.81 gap is explained by this mismatch.

I-601 is **infrastructure, not model improvement**. Goal: realign local val with what the leaderboard actually measures so future ablations stop being decided by a misleading metric. Expected leaderboard delta ≈ 0 (some chance of small ± move from best-epoch selection differences).

## Outcome

**Negative vs expectation.** Predicted ≈ baseline-0 (0.81147), got **-0.01220 leaderboard**.

Two competing explanations:
1. **Best-epoch drift**: timestamp val picked epoch ≈ 8 (step 29288), baseline-0 picked epoch 6 (step 21744). Cold restart fires every epoch, so later epochs sit on a less-trained sparse-embedding state. Picking later → worse leaderboard despite "better" timestamp val_auc.
2. **Premise wrong**: `pcvr_diag_data_split` showed all 1000 parquets cover the same ~4.6-day window, but maybe the within-window timestamp gradient is non-trivial. Last-10%-by-timestamp may not be equivalent to last-10%-by-file after all — the new val could itself overfit to a thin time slice.

Either way, the val infrastructure fix did NOT recover the 0.046 train→leaderboard gap as predicted. The gap is structural (high-card ID non-recurrence + 313% conversion-rate drift), not just split-induced.

I-601 still sets the **correct frame** for I-602 (treats id_dropout as the lever vs. a controlled timestamp-split background).
