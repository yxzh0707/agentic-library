## Rationale

I-643 has held the LB ceiling 0.824608 against 5 attack experiments (multi-cate
stack, UserPair, dow stack, alpha sweep, chorus funnel — all -0.001 to -0.005).
The 5 attacks all targeted the **feature axis**. The model is at a tight local
optimum where stacking new components fails. The **training-dynamics axis** has
not been touched on this base — I-648 only did flat lr×2 (KILL).

SORT (Alibaba International, March 2026) reports a small-base 2-layer
Transformer ranking model trained with `AdamW(betas=(0.9, 0.99), weight_decay=0.01,
lr=5e-4)`. Our current configuration uses `betas=(0.9, 0.98)`. The β2 difference
(0.98 vs 0.99) is small in absolute terms but materially affects how AdamW's
second-moment estimate forgets old gradients.

**Mechanism (Codex-corrected 2026-05-09)**:

β2 is AdamW's exponential decay rate for the second-moment estimate. **Higher
β2 = LONGER memory horizon**, not shorter:
- β2=0.999 (PyTorch default for AdamW): half-life ≈ 693 steps
- β2=0.99 (SORT recipe): half-life ≈ 69 steps
- β2=0.98 (our current I-643): half-life ≈ 35 steps

So `0.98 → 0.99` moves toward LESS forgetful, LONGER history. The original
v1 rationale claimed the opposite — that's a directional bug, fixed here.

**Why higher β2 (longer memory) might help on our setup**:
- Cold restart resets sparse Adagrad state every epoch. Dense AdamW reads
  through wiped embedding rows. The first-order signal (β1=0.9) responds
  fast; β2 is where second-order curvature info accumulates.
- At our batch size 256, gradient noise is high. β2=0.98 (35-step half-life)
  may be discarding curvature too fast, leading to noisier per-step
  preconditioning. SORT's β2=0.99 (69-step half-life) doubles the memory
  for the second-moment denominator, which **stabilizes** preconditioning
  under noisy gradients without going all the way to default 0.999.
- This is the OPPOSITE of the v1 "forget faster" story. Empirically SORT
  picked 0.99 on a structurally-similar 2-layer Transformer ranker; we are
  testing whether the same point transfers, not whether a specific
  forgetfulness narrative holds.

**Why-Warmup theory (arXiv 2510.03164, Oct 2025) is tangentially relevant**:
proves warmup helps when loss landscape is ill-conditioned. Cold restart
creates such a landscape, but β2 tuning addresses second-moment stability,
not gradient magnitude — different lever from warmup. **The two are
complementary, not substitutes**, contra v1's claim.

## Risk Audit

- **β2=0.99 too forgetful**: would manifest as oscillating loss in epoch 1.
  Mitigation: log per-step grad-norm and AdamW second-moment max during the
  first 200 steps; manual kill if grad-norm > 5× I-643's normal range.
- **Multi-epoch interaction unclear**: SORT trained 1 epoch. We train 6+.
  If β2=0.99 backfires it'll show as worse epoch 2 LB than epoch 1 (the
  opposite of I-643's monotonic improvement to epoch 6). Not a hard kill
  signal but worth watching.
- **Pattern A check**: training-dynamics axis is feature-agnostic →
  inherently OOV-safe. No item_id binding introduced.
- **Closed-axis check**: NOT a reweighting/pairwise/focal variant. NOT a
  capacity/gating/feature change. Lands cleanly on axis (c) training
  dynamics from `feedback_meta_rule_dual_condition.md` — free pass.
